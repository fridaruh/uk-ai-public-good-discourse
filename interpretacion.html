1The project and its guiding principle

This repository operationalizes the thesis's discourse analysis: tracing how the framing “AI for the public good” functions as a sociotechnical imaginary in UK government discourse on AI in public services, anchored in the Government Digital Service. The frozen v1 corpus brings together 35 documents published between January 2024 and July 2026: the digital centre's strategy-and-guidance trunk (CDDO → GDS) alongside DSIT, and the five Memorandum of Understanding families between DSIT and frontier AI companies (Anthropic, Cohere, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, ElevenLabs), each with its government and corporate press materials. The whole pipeline is bound by the three specific objectives declared in PLAN.md: SO1 (the imaginary projected in GDS texts), SO2 (the trajectory from declared principle to policy commitment and public claim), and SO3 (how the framing evolves across the partnership documents). No component exists unless it serves one of these; whatever only validates the method lives in analysis/qa/ and is marked as internal QA.

Guiding principle: the LLM locates and extracts; the author interprets and consolidates. The scripts find where the term and its variants appear, segment the passage, extract verifiable verbatim quotes, and propose groupings. Nothing the model produces is a finding: it is located raw material. Naming a sub-code, validating a metaphorical domain, deciding what a zero count means, and reading the intertextual network are interpretive acts performed by the researcher, and the pipeline is built to always keep that boundary visible (the retrieval field, quote_verified checks, and the DRAFT and auto_provisional labels).

Strategy and guidance trunk (family = None) — 18 documents, chronological order

From the CDDO's generative AI framework to the Action Plan's one-year review: the line along which the nominal term appears, gets defined, and travels across genres (strategy → ministerial statement → blog → press release).

2024-01-18_STRAT_CDDO_GenerativeAIFramework
2024-01-18STRAT CDDOpresentT1
Central Digital and Data Office — not yet GDS at that date

Generative AI framework for HM Government: the government's first practical guidance for building generative AI solutions, with ten principles and sections on data, security, procurement, and governance. It speaks to civil servants, not the public: the register is that of a technical manual, and the benefit is framed as productivity and savings “to taxpayers”. It is the trunk's starting point and the document the AI Playbook supersedes in 2025 — the only supersession edge declared in the network.

2024-02-06_REG_DSIT_ProInnovationAIRegulation
2024-02-06REG DSITpresentT3
DSIT — response to a consultation laid before Parliament (CP 1019)

The government's response to the white paper consultation A pro-innovation approach to AI regulation: it consolidates the sector-based, principles-driven approach and introduces the AI Safety Institute as a body “committed to advancing AI safety in the public interest”. This is the previous government's document and the regulatory anchor for the pre-July-2024 period; with 17 incoming references it is the corpus's second most-cited node. It earns its place because it fixes the vocabulary (public interest, pro-innovation) against which everything later is read.

2025-01-12_PRGOV_PMO_BlueprintTurbochargeAI
2025-01-12PRGOV PMO (No. 10)presentT3
Prime Minister's Office, 10 Downing Street, and DSIT

A press release announcing the government's adoption of the AI Opportunities Action Plan's 50 recommendations. It is the trunk's most metaphor-dense document (turbocharge, unleashed, “mainlines AI into the veins of this enterprising nation”), and the one with the most explicitly distributive beneficiary marking: working people, “more money in people’s back pockets”. It uses the nominal term only once against five distributive markers — a clear case for reading the shift from the noun toward a promise of distribution.

CONTEXT_2025-01-13_STRAT_DSIT_AIOpportunitiesActionPlan
2025-01-13STRAT External adviserabsentT3
Matt Clifford CBE, the PM's AI adviser, commissioned by DSIT · is_context = True

AI Opportunities Action Plan: the 50 recommendations that reorganize the Labour government's AI policy — compute infrastructure, AI Growth Zones, the National Data Library, sector-by-sector adoption, “Scan > Pilot > Scale”. It is by far the corpus's most-cited document (44 incoming references): nearly the whole of the later strategy, and nearly every MoU, invokes it as an authoritative source. It never uses the nominal form public good, and that absence in the most-cited text is itself a data point for SO1/SO2.

2025-01-13_STRAT_DSIT_AIActionPlanGovResponse
2025-01-13STRAT DSITvariantT2
DSIT — formal response laid before Parliament (CP 1242), with a foreword by the PM

The official response endorsing Clifford's 50 recommendations. Keir Starmer's foreword condenses the whole plot: AI as the “defining opportunity of our generation”, already here and “materially changing lives”, against a government that “will not sit back passively” and whose responsibility is “to harness it and make it work for working people”. A hinge document: it turns an external adviser's report into a government commitment.

2025-01-21_WMS_DSIT_BlueprintMinisterialStatement
2025-01-21WMS DSITpresentT2
Written ministerial statement, House of Commons

The presentation to Parliament of the State of Digital Government Review and the Blueprint. It sets out the six-point plan and contains the cleanest formulation of the term in its programmatic form: “We will harness the power of AI for the public good”, followed by the three commitments that operationalize it (an AI adoption unit, capability across government, trust and accountability). It is the reference passage for the phrase's definitional genealogy, and, by genre (WMS), the point where the imaginary is addressed directly to Parliament.

2025-01-21_STRAT_GDS_StateOfDigitalGovReview
2025-01-21STRAT DSIT + GDSpresentT1
DSIT and Government Digital Service (CP 1251)

A diagnosis of the public sector's digital state: satisfaction falling from 79% to 68%, 47% of central services with no digital route, £45 billion a year in unrealized savings, five root causes. This is the document that constructs the problem to which AI will later be presented as the answer, and the source of nearly every figure that later circulates through press and blogs (13 incoming references). Without this text the Blueprint's narrative need makes no sense.

2025-01-21_STRAT_GDS_BlueprintModernDigitalGov
2025-01-21STRAT DSIT + GDSpresentT1
DSIT and GDS — “the digital centre”, named GDS in this document (CP 1252)

A blueprint for modern digital government: the six-point plan of the reconstituted digital centre, with “harness the power of AI for the public good” as one of its named pillars. It is the corpus's document with the most nominal occurrences (9), and the one where the phrase holds a structural, pillar-level position rather than a prose one: that structural position is itself a finding declared by the design. Core to SO1.

2025-01-27_BLOG_GDS_SameNameNewAmbitions
2025-01-27BLOG GDSabsentT1
Joanna Davinson, Government Chief Digital Officer, GDS

An institutional blog explaining the rebrand: GDS, CDDO, i.AI, the geospatial team, and the Responsible Technology Adoption Unit merge under the name GDS. It translates the Blueprint into a practitioner register (“services should do more of the hard work on people’s behalf”) and uses neither the term nor its variant, despite being pure GDS authorship. A high-value zero count for SO2: the same actor, the same week, addressing a different audience, without the phrase.

2025-02-10_STRAT_GDS_AIPlaybookUKGovernment
2025-02-10STRAT GDSpresentT1
Government Digital Service — 1,123 blocks, the corpus's longest document

Artificial Intelligence Playbook for the UK Government: it supersedes and extends the CDDO's Generative AI Framework to the full range of AI technologies. The changelog explicitly declares a new section titled “Societal wellbeing and public good”: it is the only document in the corpus where the phrase becomes the heading of an operational guidance section, with 9 variant occurrences against 4 nominal ones. A central piece for reading what the phrase demands once it has to become an instruction.

2025-02-10_BLOG_GDS_LaunchingAIPlaybook
2025-02-10BLOG GDSabsentT1
Government Digital Service

The announcement of the Playbook's launch: who it serves, what it contains, how it links to Civil Service Learning courses. The benefit is framed as “putting AI to good use — making things run better […] and giving the economy a boost”, without using either the nominal form or the variant. A direct comparison pair with the Playbook it announces: the same content loses the phrase when it moves from guidance to blog.

2025-02-14_PRGOV_DSIT_TacklingAISecurityRisks
2025-02-14PRGOV DSITabsentT3
DSIT, AI Security Institute, Rt Hon Peter Kyle MP

A press release renaming the AI Safety Institute as the AI Security Institute, creating a criminal-misuse team with the Home Office, and announcing, in the same release, the agreement with Anthropic. The lexical shift from safety to security — and the clarification that the institute “will not focus on bias or freedom of speech” — is a THREAT_TYPE move observable within a single text. A bridge document between the trunk and the Anthropic family.

2025-08-18_BLOG_GDS_AIExemplarsProgramme
2025-08-18BLOG GDSpresentT1
Government Digital Service

Explains the Prime Minister's AI Exemplars Programme as a “portfolio approach”: several simultaneous use cases, fast learning from successes and failures, the “Scan > Pilot > Scale” framework taken from the Action Plan. Here the phrase appears in its shortened form, AI for public good (no article), inside a portfolio-management text — useful for tracking the term's banalization as it becomes a programme label.

2026-01-19_WMS_DSIT_RoadmapMinisterialStatement
2026-01-19WMS DSIT + GDSabsentT1
DSIT minister addressing the Commons, on behalf of DSIT and GDS

A written ministerial statement presenting the Roadmap for modern digital government 2025-2030: the National Data Library, procurement reform, algorithmic transparency, and an inventory of achievements (the GOV.UK app, GOV.UK Wallet, One Login with 13.2 million people). A decisive contrast: the 2025 WMS opened with “AI for the public good”, and the 2026 WMS — same genre, same parliamentary audience — does not contain the phrase. It is the corpus's cleanest pair for SO2's temporal argument.

2026-01-20_STRAT_GDS_RoadmapModernDigitalGov
2026-01-20STRAT DSIT + GDSpresentT1
GDS and DSIT — campaign site, six pillars

The roadmap as a web product: a message from Secretary of State Liz Kendall (“politics should tread lightly on people’s lives”) and six pillars, one of them the direct heir to the Blueprint's AI pillar. A short, tightly structured document: the pillar_name fields let the phrase be read in heading position and compared with the Blueprint's a year earlier.

2026-01-20_BLOG_GDS_OurRoadmapLaunch
2026-01-20BLOG GDSpresentT1
Emily Middleton and Christine Bellamy, Director Generals, GDS

The roadmap's launch blog, with a review of the Blueprint's first year: kickstarters, the GOV.UK app, Wallet with the Veteran Card, One Login, the new CustomerFirst team. Its second heading is literally “Harnessing AI for public good” — the only time in the corpus that the phrase heads a blog section. Comparing it with SameNameNewAmbitions (2025), the equivalent blog that did not use it, supports the reading that the term settles into the GDS register a year late.

2026-01-27_PRGOV_DSIT_TopBritishAIExpertise
2026-01-27PRGOV DSITpresentT3
DSIT — press release, Minister Ian Murray

Announces a cohort of AI fellows funded with $1 million from Meta through the Alan Turing Institute, and, in the same release, the start of the collaboration with Anthropic on the GOV.UK assistant. It uses the phrase in its most ceremonial form: the action week shows how the technology “are being put to work for the public good, benefitting hardworking people at the point of delivery”. The government's twin release to Anthropic's own on the same day: a required pair for echo-phrases.

2026-01-29_STRAT_DSIT_AIActionPlanOneYearOn
2026-01-29STRAT DSITvariantT2
DSIT — one-year review, forewords by the PM and the Secretary of State

The Action Plan's accountability review, organized around three axes (“Putting people first”, “Rewiring the state”, “Supercharging the economy”) with delivery figures: 2.4 million AI-assisted X-rays, five AI Growth Zones, £2 billion in compute, the Sovereign AI Unit. It closes out the corpus's timeline on the strategy side. The text sustains the distributive promise (“benefits everyone”, “work for everyone”) without resorting to the nominal form — a variant case in a genre that previously carried present.

coding/units.jsonl — 53 units across 35 documents

A coding unit is a section: the contiguous blocks under the same top-level heading. It is the literal implementation of the declared method — a full scan of the document, detailed coding of the section that contains the term — and it avoids both the decontextualized passage and the whole document as a unit.

Each line is a JSON object with unit_id (<doc_id>::sNN or ::full), doc_id, heading, block_ids, text, retrieval, and the hits_nominal, hits_variant, hits_distributive lists holding the literal matches.

retrievalnHow the unit was retrievedWhat it implies when interpreting
lexicon36The section contains at least one match from the lexicon v1 (nominal, variant, or distributive).Direct, auditable lexical evidence: the exact matches are in the record itself.
semantic3The document has no nominal form; up to 2 sections were retrieved by cosine similarity against the beneficiary probes (embeddinggemma, 0.52 threshold). Carries a similarity field.It is a proposal of where the distributive claim might be, not a detection. Verify the passage before quoting it; the zero count comes annotated with “and in its place it says X”.
full_short_doc14A short document (≤ 9,000 characters) with no prior unit: the whole document is the unit.Guarantees SO3 coverage: the intra-family comparison needs every MoU and press release, whether or not it carries the term. The unit is the whole text, not a section.

Careful reading note: size measures how many times a document is cited within the corpus, not its overall importance. The AI Opportunities Action Plan (44 entries) and the State of Digital Government Review (13) dominate the view because they are the sources everyone invokes.

Expected format for each record

{doc_id, passage_id, question, so_tags, answer_summary, verbatim_quote, applies, confidence, model, prompt_version, run_id, timestamp, quote_verified}

quote_verified

quote_verified is the result of checking, character by character, that verbatim_quote literally exists in the passage's text. It is the best hallucination detector available for this task: if it is false, the record is retried or discarded, and no unverified quote can make it into the thesis. When reading Round 1, always filter by quote_verified == true before citing.

Fields that are NOT findings

answer_summary is the model's own description, not a validated interpretation; confidence is self-reported by the model and is useful for prioritizing review, not as a quality measure. The only thing citable without mediation is verbatim_quote with quote_verified == true.

The definitional extractor runs separately, into coding/round1/definitional_instances.jsonl: statements where the document says what the phrase means or requires, with their structural position. The pipeline only aligns them chronologically; reading what each definition keeps, drops, adds, or replaces is the author's analysis.

coding/validation/sample_for_author.csv in progress (Phase 4)

Does not exist yet. It will be the sample for the double coding that supports the methods chapter: 15–20% of the passages, stratified by genre and family.

How the double coding is done

  1. The CSV arrives with the context columns (unit_id, doc_id, genre, family, question, passage_text) and one empty column to code the answer into. The LLM's answer is hidden or kept in a separate file: coding blind is what makes the agreement valid.
  2. The author codes her column without looking at the model's.
  3. The agreement script merges both and reports concordance per question, not globally: agreement on BENEFICIARY and agreement on NATURALISED_ORDER are not comparable and should not be averaged.
  4. Disagreements feed at most one round of prompt iteration. After that the prompts are frozen: if you iterate until the model agrees, the agreement measure stops measuring anything.
  5. The agreement report lives in coding/validation/ and is nearly drop-in text for the methods chapter.

4Assumptions and technical limitations

Read this section before writing any claim from this data. Everything that follows is either a decision that was made or a real constraint of the method, not an accident.

Corpus and admission rules

The v1 corpus has 35 documents, not 37

The design anticipated 37 decided rows in the Official_Document Selection sheet. Reading the range that was actually decided (up to but not including the “EITHER BRING COHERE…” row), the real count is 35: there is one empty row inside the range. No document was removed and no additional filter was applied. Any mention of “37 documents” in earlier material — including the heading of PLAN.md — is out of date; the correct figure for the thesis is 35.

The CONTEXT_ document enters the corpus, with speaker “external adviser”

The AI Opportunities Action Plan was written by Matt Clifford CBE, the Prime Minister's AI adviser, commissioned by the DSIT Secretary of State with departmental support. By the author's decision, 2026-08-29, it enters the corpus coded with Speaker = External_adviser, side = "Public, led by external actor", and is_context = True. Analytical consequence: it is the corpus's most-cited node (44 incoming references) and it is not government text speaking in the first person. Any claim about “government discourse” that leans on this node must declare that condition.

gds_tier is a provisional automatic assignment

The rule applied by 04_segment.py is mechanical: T1 if speaker is GDS, CDDO, or DSIT_and_GDS; T2 if the text mentions GDS, Government Digital Service, i.AI, Incubator for AI, AI Playbook, or Service Standard; T3 in every other case. All 35 rows carry gds_tier_source = auto_provisional. The rule is deliberately literal and therefore brittle: it cannot distinguish a passing mention from an attribution of responsibility. The PublicGood_Nominal × GDSTier query cannot be reported until the author reviews it row by row.

Term detection and retrieval

The v0 intertextual network detects references by title alias

06_network_v0.py looks for mentions of other corpus documents' titles (and their common aliases) within each document's text. This detects the nominal citation well — “the AI Opportunities Action Plan”, “the Blueprint” — and systematically underestimates paraphrase: a document that reproduces another's argument without naming it generates no edge. The 51 edges are therefore a floor, not a census. The Phase 6 version adds echo-phrases (n-grams ≥ 6 words) and shared codes, which capture part of what this version misses.

term_status was resolved using the lexicon v1

lexicon_v1.yaml is regex, not NLP. Stemming is switched off by design, following the NVivo plan's rule: the nominal pattern is public good(?!s), so that “goods” is excluded and doesn't contaminate the count. Three layers: nominal (public good, the public good, AI for (the) public good) → present; nominal variant (public benefit, public interest, societal wellbeing, social good) → variant; distributive (working people, taxpayer, benefit all/everyone, improve people’s lives…), which is counted but does not change the status. Known limitation: the distributive layer is generous — taxpayer shows up in purely accounting contexts — and its counts should be read as a signal, not a measurement. Any change to the lexicon produces a v2; it is never edited in place.

The semantic threshold of 0.52 is empirically calibrated

Semantic retrieval uses embeddinggemma via Ollama, with three beneficiary probes and a cosine threshold of 0.52, up to 2 passages per document. The threshold is not a canonical value: it was calibrated over the absent documents by observing that true positives fall in the 0.52–0.63 range on that model's scale. Switching embedding models invalidates the threshold. The resulting 3 semantic units are tagged with their similarity and must be verified by hand before being used as evidence.

Capture, archiving, and text characteristics

The Cohere document required JS rendering

Cohere's site, and its snapshots, serve an empty page without JavaScript. The text of 2025-06-15_PRCO_Cohere_CanadaUKPartnerships was obtained through JS rendering (r.jina.ai) over the direct URL. The content matches the public page, but the retrieval route differs from the rest and must be disclosed if the committee asks about reproducibility.

The two parliament.uk documents were captured via browser

2025-01-21_WMS_DSIT_BlueprintMinisterialStatement and 2026-01-19_WMS_DSIT_RoadmapMinisterialStatement sit behind Cloudflare and were captured with a browser (source = browser_prefetch). They do not refresh automatically: if those texts change at the source, the pipeline will not detect it. A local copy exists in data/raw/.

DeepMind's two press releases carry no attributed quotes

n_quotes = 0 in 2025-12-10_PRCO_DeepMind_StrengtheningPartnership and 2025-12-11_PRCO_DeepMind_DeepeningAISIPartnership. This is a real feature of the text, not an extraction failure: DeepMind publishes in an institutional first-person-plural voice, with no statements attributed to named individuals — unlike OpenAI, ElevenLabs, or the government releases, which do include them. It is a data point for AGENCY and for SO3's inter-family comparison, not a gap in the corpus.

Archive.org snapshots: 33 of 35

Every document has a snapshot except two, for which archive.org returned none: the Blueprint's WMS (2025-01-21) and the ElevenLabs MoU (2026-06-08). Both have a local copy in data/raw/. For the remaining 33 the status is partial: a prior snapshot exists but no new one could be generated during this run. The full inventory, with timestamps, is in data/raw/archive_urls.json. data/raw/ is in .gitignore: the originals live on disk and on archive.org, not in the repository.

LLM-assisted coding

Round 1 is LLM-assisted and is not a final result

Round 1 coding runs on Ollama Cloud, with temperature 0, forced JSON, and automatic verification of every quote against the source text. The documents are public, so there is no confidentiality concern; the provider, exact model, and date are logged all the same, and every run carries a run_id, prompt version, and guidebook version, so any number in the thesis can be traced back to a specific run.

None of Round 1 counts as a result until two things happen: (1) the author's validation over the 15–20% sample, with its per-question agreement report, and (2) her consolidation of the guidebook in Phase 5. Before that, Round 1 is passage location, not coding.

The sub-codes and metaphor domains are suggestions

The clusters in guidebook_draft.yaml and the source/target pairs in metaphors_report.md arrive explicitly marked as proposals. A semantic cluster groups similar answers; it does not say what they have in common conceptually, and that is precisely the analytical operation. New codes are never born without a human decision: in Phase 7, answers with no existing sub-code accumulate as candidate_code for review — they are never created automatically.
