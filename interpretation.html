1The project and its guiding principle

This repository operationalizes the thesis's discourse analysis: tracing how the framing “AI for the public good” functions as a sociotechnical imaginary in UK government discourse on AI in public services, anchored in the Government Digital Service. The frozen v1 corpus brings together 35 documents published between January 2024 and July 2026: the digital centre's strategy-and-guidance trunk (CDDO → GDS) alongside DSIT, and the five Memorandum of Understanding families between DSIT and frontier AI companies (Anthropic, Cohere, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, ElevenLabs), each with its government and corporate press materials. On 2026-09-10 a Phase 7 incremental intake added 31 further documents — three brand-new MoU-partner families (NVIDIA, Cisco, Synthesia), six trunk/WMS gap-fillers, and 17 departmental and local-government blog posts — bringing the corpus to 66 documents without touching v1: filtering data/manifest.csv by corpus_version == 1 still reproduces the original 35-document analysis exactly. The whole pipeline is bound by the three specific objectives declared in PLAN.md: SO1 (the imaginary projected in GDS texts), SO2 (the trajectory from declared principle to policy commitment and public claim), and SO3 (how the framing evolves across the partnership documents). No component exists unless it serves one of these; whatever only validates the method lives in analysis/qa/ and is marked as internal QA.

Guiding principle: the LLM locates and extracts; the author interprets and consolidates. The scripts find where the term and its variants appear, segment the passage, extract verifiable verbatim quotes, and propose groupings. Nothing the model produces is a finding: it is located raw material. Naming a sub-code, validating a metaphorical domain, deciding what a zero count means, and reading the intertextual network are interpretive acts performed by the researcher, and the pipeline is built to always keep that boundary visible (the retrieval field, quote_verified checks, and the DRAFT and auto_provisional labels).

Strategy and guidance trunk (family = None) — 18 documents, chronological order

From the CDDO's generative AI framework to the Action Plan's one-year review: the line along which the nominal term appears, gets defined, and travels across genres (strategy → ministerial statement → blog → press release).

2024-01-18_STRAT_CDDO_GenerativeAIFramework
2024-01-18STRAT CDDOpresentT1
Central Digital and Data Office — not yet GDS at that date

Generative AI framework for HM Government: the government's first practical guidance for building generative AI solutions, with ten principles and sections on data, security, procurement, and governance. It speaks to civil servants, not the public: the register is that of a technical manual, and the benefit is framed as productivity and savings “to taxpayers”. It is the trunk's starting point and the document the AI Playbook supersedes in 2025 — the only supersession edge declared in the network.

2024-02-06_REG_DSIT_ProInnovationAIRegulation
2024-02-06REG DSITpresentT3
DSIT — response to a consultation laid before Parliament (CP 1019)

The government's response to the white paper consultation A pro-innovation approach to AI regulation: it consolidates the sector-based, principles-driven approach and introduces the AI Safety Institute as a body “committed to advancing AI safety in the public interest”. This is the previous government's document and the regulatory anchor for the pre-July-2024 period; with 17 incoming references it is the corpus's second most-cited node. It earns its place because it fixes the vocabulary (public interest, pro-innovation) against which everything later is read.

2025-01-12_PRGOV_PMO_BlueprintTurbochargeAI
2025-01-12PRGOV PMO (No. 10)presentT3
Prime Minister's Office, 10 Downing Street, and DSIT

A press release announcing the government's adoption of the AI Opportunities Action Plan's 50 recommendations. It is the trunk's most metaphor-dense document (turbocharge, unleashed, “mainlines AI into the veins of this enterprising nation”), and the one with the most explicitly distributive beneficiary marking: working people, “more money in people’s back pockets”. It uses the nominal term only once against five distributive markers — a clear case for reading the shift from the noun toward a promise of distribution.

2025-01-13STRAT External adviserabsentT3
Matt Clifford CBE, the PM's AI adviser, commissioned by DSIT · is_context = True

AI Opportunities Action Plan: the 50 recommendations that reorganize the Labour government's AI policy — compute infrastructure, AI Growth Zones, the National Data Library, sector-by-sector adoption, “Scan > Pilot > Scale”. It is by far the corpus's most-cited document (44 incoming references): nearly the whole of the later strategy, and nearly every MoU, invokes it as an authoritative source. The official response endorsing Clifford's 50 recommendations. The presentation to Parliament of the State of Digital Government Review and the Blueprint. 2cTerm defined vs. term used

The author's own manual notes (NVIVO CODING R1.docx) asked, for every document in v1, both "'AI for public good' is defined as: ____" and "...is used as: ____". Read across all 35 entries, the first line is never filled in; the second always is. This section makes that observation systematic and corpus-wide: it reruns the same question (locate an explicit DEFINITIONAL instance — a passage that states what the phrase means or requires, not merely uses it) as a coded field (DEFINITIONAL in prompts_v1.yaml) over all 66 documents, in both coding runs, and cross-checks the result against the author's manual reading.

Finding. Of the 23 documents (out of 66) that carry the nominal phrase or a named variant, only 4 documents (17%), 5 instances, produce an explicit DEFINITIONAL record. Four of those five gloss the term via a mechanism, criterion, or list of actions; the fifth — found in a 2026-09-13 re-pass specifically targeting the umbrella term's own surface forms — is genuinely conceptual. The other 19 use the phrase (or "public benefit" / "public interest" / "societal wellbeing") as a citation of itself: a label the reader is assumed to already understand. This matches — and sharpens with a corpus-wide count — what the author's own manual first-pass coding already suggested by leaving "is defined as" blank throughout.

Expected format for each record

{doc_id, passage_id, question, so_tags, answer_summary, verbatim_quote, applies, confidence, model, prompt_version, run_id, timestamp, quote_verified}

quote_verified

quote_verified is the result of checking, character by character, that verbatim_quote literally exists in the passage's text. It is the best hallucination detector available for this task: if it is false, the record is retried or discarded, and no unverified quote can make it into the thesis. When reading Round 1, always filter by quote_verified == true before citing.

Fields that are NOT findings

answer_summary is the model's own description, not a validated interpretation; confidence is self-reported by the model and is useful for prioritizing review, not as a quality measure. The only thing citable without mediation is verbatim_quote with quote_verified == true.

The definitional extractor runs separately, into coding/round1/definitional_instances.jsonl (4 instances, chronological): statements where the document says what the phrase means or requires, with their structural position — see the dedicated Term defined vs. term used section for the full reading. The pipeline only aligns them chronologically; reading what each definition keeps, drops, adds, or replaces is the author's analysis.

How the double coding is done

  1. The CSV arrives with the context columns (unit_id, doc_id, genre, family, question, passage_text) and one empty column to code the answer into. The LLM's answer is hidden or kept in a separate file: coding blind is what makes the agreement valid.
  2. The author codes her column without looking at the model's.
  3. The agreement script merges both and reports concordance per question, not globally: agreement on BENEFICIARY and agreement on NATURALISED_ORDER are not comparable and should not be averaged.
  4. Disagreements feed at most one round of prompt iteration. After that the prompts are frozen: if you iterate until the model agrees, the agreement measure stops measuring anything.
  5. The agreement report lives in coding/validation/ and is nearly drop-in text for the methods chapter.

Term detection and retrieval

The v0 intertextual network detects references by title alias

06_network_v0.py looks for mentions of other corpus documents' titles (and their common aliases) within each document's text. This detects the nominal citation well — “the AI Opportunities Action Plan”, “the Blueprint” — and systematically underestimates paraphrase: a document that reproduces another's argument without naming it generates no edge. The 51 edges are therefore a floor, not a census. The Phase 6 version adds echo-phrases (n-grams ≥ 6 words) and shared codes, which capture part of what this version misses.

term_status was resolved using the lexicon v1

lexicon_v1.yaml is regex, not NLP. Stemming is switched off by design, following the NVivo plan's rule: the nominal pattern is public good(?!s), so that “goods” is excluded and doesn't contaminate the count. Three layers: nominal (public good, the public good, AI for (the) public good) → present; nominal variant (public benefit, public interest, societal wellbeing, social good) → variant; distributive (working people, taxpayer, benefit all/everyone, improve people’s lives…), which is counted but does not change the status. Known limitation: the distributive layer is generous — taxpayer shows up in purely accounting contexts — and its counts should be read as a signal, not a measurement. Any change to the lexicon produces a v2; it is never edited in place.

The semantic threshold of 0.52 is empirically calibrated

Semantic retrieval uses embeddinggemma via Ollama, with three beneficiary probes and a cosine threshold of 0.52, up to 2 passages per document. The threshold is not a canonical value: it was calibrated over the absent documents by observing that true positives fall in the 0.52–0.63 range on that model's scale. Switching embedding models invalidates the threshold. The resulting 3 semantic units are tagged with their similarity and must be verified by hand before being used as evidence.

LLM-assisted coding

One coding pipeline, two Ollama models, and one retired interim pass

All 66 documents are now coded entirely by Ollama Cloud: v1's 35 documents by kimi-k3:cloud, and the Phase 7 intake's 31 documents by a mix of kimi-k3:cloud and, once the session usage quota was hit mid-run, deepseek-v4-flash:cloud. The model field on every record is what made the interim pass traceable and replaceable in the first place: grep '"model": "claude-code-local"' now returns nothing anywhere in coding/round1/.

Round 1 is LLM-assisted and is not a final result

Round 1 coding runs entirely on Ollama Cloud, with temperature 0, forced JSON, and automatic verification of every quote against the source text. The documents are public, so there is no confidentiality concern; the provider, exact model, and date are logged all the same, and every run carries a run_id, prompt version, and guidebook version, so any number in the thesis can be traced back to a specific run.

None of Round 1 counts as a result until two things happen: (1) the author's validation over the 15–20% sample, with its per-question agreement report, and (2) her consolidation of the guidebook in Phase 5. Before that, Round 1 is passage location, not coding.

The sub-codes and metaphor domains are suggestions

The clusters in guidebook_draft.yaml and the source/target pairs in metaphors_report.md arrive explicitly marked as proposals. A semantic cluster groups similar answers; it does not say what they have in common conceptually, and that is precisely the analytical operation. New codes are never born without a human decision: in Phase 7, answers with no existing sub-code accumulate as candidate_code for review — they are never created automatically.
